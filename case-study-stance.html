01

THE PRODUCT OPPORTUNITY.

Specialist insurance needs to feel understandable before it can feel trustworthy. The job was to turn Stance’s proposition into a confident, focused digital starting point.

Design question

How might we explain cover for a personal, highly customised purchase without making the customer work through insurance language first?

Design principle

Lead with the driver and the car. Keep the primary action visible, make the value proposition scannable and let the detail support the decision.

02

AN END-TO-END DESIGN PATH.

This project was structured as a practical product-design cycle, from framing through responsive delivery.

01Frame

Clarify the audience, the product promise and the one action the landing experience needs to earn.

02Structure

Order the information so a customer can understand the offer before they encounter the details.

03Design

Establish hierarchy, visual language, components and interaction states across the key screens.

04Adapt

Check that the hierarchy and call to action survive desktop, tablet and mobile layouts.

05Handover

Document reusable patterns and responsive intent so implementation retains the product logic.

03

ONE JOURNEY, THREE VIEWPORTS.

Use the image controls to focus on the desktop, tablet or mobile direction. The key test is not whether the screens match; it is whether the customer can still recognise the offer and find the next step.

04

THE UI SYSTEM IN PRACTICE.

The visual direction uses an approachable, enthusiast-led tone without losing the clarity expected from an insurance product.

What stays consistent

A clear value proposition, generous space around the core message, and a visible call to action create a stable path across every layout.

What changes by device

Content density and composition adjust to the available space. The task and language do not: the experience remains recognisably Stance at every breakpoint.

05

06

THE NEXT ITERATION.

A case study should show where the work can continue, not pretend a launch is the finish line.

Where I would take it next

  • Test the product language with a broader mix of drivers and first-time buyers.
  • Extend the design system into quoting, policy management and claims so the promise continues after the landing page.
  • Instrument the core journey before making optimisation claims: entry source, quote start, quote completion and support contact.
